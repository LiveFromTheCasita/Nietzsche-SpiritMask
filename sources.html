Texts, translations, and further reading

This guide cites books and section numbers so you can use your own edition. We prefer Walter Kaufmann and R. J. Hollingdale where the editions below cover the assigned work. Publisher links provide edition and purchase information; ask your library about borrowing the named edition. Free online alternatives remain available through Project Gutenberg. Their wording and editorial notes belong to the credited translators, not to Kaufmann or Hollingdale.

How to find a passage

These are edition recommendations, not replacement texts hosted on this site. Existing quotations retain their stated sources. Page numbers vary: follow the work, part or essay, and section number. Chapter headings may differ. “Overman” and “superman” translate Übermensch; “Apollinian” and “Apollonian” refer to the same term. Check the chapter’s position and neighboring headings when its English title differs.

§ means section; §§ means several sections. In the Genealogy, a Roman numeral identifies the essay: III §28 means the third essay, section 28. In Zarathustra, use the part and chapter title. Translation choices can change titles: “Self-Overcoming” may appear as “Self-Surpassing,” for example.

Some links open near the assigned section; others open the book or chapter. Use the contents or your browser’s Find command to locate the number or title in the assignment. A reading step can include several passages.

Further reading and disagreements

These books represent different approaches. They are further reading, not a claim that every interpretation on this site has been reviewed by their authors.

  • Walter Kaufmann, Nietzsche: Philosopher, Psychologist, Antichrist. Princeton University Press. The interpretive tradition identified in this guide’s About page.
  • Maudemarie Clark, Nietzsche on Truth and Philosophy. Cambridge University Press. Consult especially the chapters on perspectivism and will to power.
  • Brian Leiter, Nietzsche on Morality, second edition. Routledge. An approach to Nietzsche through naturalism and moral psychology.
  • Hugo Drochon, Nietzsche’s Great Politics. Princeton University Press. Read the introduction (PDF) for a challenge to an exclusively apolitical interpretation.

Compare an interpretation with the complete passage and its neighbors. An explanation on this site is an argument about the text; the citation gives you a way to test it.
