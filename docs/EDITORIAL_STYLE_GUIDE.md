# Editorial Style Guide

## Governing voice
The Spirit Mask should sound like a careful reader guiding another careful reader through Nietzsche.

The voice is:
- serious but readable;
- philosophically literate without academic performance;
- interpretive but text-accountable;
- skeptical of easy slogans;
- willing to state Nietzsche's disturbing or anti-egalitarian claims plainly;
- willing to identify uncertainty, development, contradiction, and tension;
- explanatory without turning Nietzsche into self-help.

## Default editorial posture
Use formulations such as:
- "Nietzsche argues..."
- "This passage connects..."
- "Read alongside..."
- "This complicates..."
- "The claim still needs evidence..."
- "The passage does not automatically establish..."

Do not write as though every interpretation is settled. Distinguish Nietzsche's claim, the site's interpretation, and a factual/historical assertion.

## Avoid
- inspirational quote-page language;
- motivational "become your best self" framing;
- treating "will to power" as a universal one-line key;
- flattening Nietzsche into libertarian individualism, nihilism, fascism, existentialism, or therapy;
- sanitizing hierarchy, exploitation, cruelty, anti-democratic claims, gender claims, or polemics;
- sensationalizing those claims;
- presenting notebook material as if Nietzsche published it as a finished book;
- falsely harmonizing early and late positions.

## Paragraph style
Prefer compact paragraphs with one argumentative job each. Lead with the interpretive question or distinction, then anchor it to a passage. Avoid long abstract introductions before readers know what text is at issue.

## Passage-card style
Each assigned passage should normally provide:
- role: Core / Development / Complication / Extension (or a clearly justified variant);
- exact citation;
- short descriptive heading;
- a genuine reading question;
- "Why it belongs" explanation;
- "Read alongside" comparison where useful;
- online edition and preferred-edition links.

The question should make the reader investigate the text, not merely repeat a fact.

## Canonical voice examples
Primary theme exemplars:
- Master and Slave Morality
- Self-Overcoming
- Overman
- The Herd and the Last Man
- Revaluation of Values

Primary book-guide exemplars:
- Beyond Good and Evil
- Twilight of the Idols
- The Antichrist

When new prose conflicts with the tone and method of these pages, revise the new prose rather than redefining the standard.

## Handling contested or troubling material
State what Nietzsche says and where. Separate:
1. textual description;
2. historical claim;
3. philosophical inference;
4. modern evaluation.

Do not hide material that complicates a preferred reading. For example, inward "nobility" does not by itself cancel passages endorsing social rank; democratic protections in one middle-period passage do not erase later anti-egalitarian claims.

## Chronology
Chronology is interpretively relevant. Name retrospective prefaces and later additions when they matter. Do not use later Nietzsche to silently rewrite earlier Nietzsche, or vice versa.

## Translation language
Keep translation differences visible when they affect titles or concepts: Übermensch/overman/superman; Apollinian/Apollonian; Self-Overcoming/Self-Surpassing. Do not imply that one English title is Nietzsche's original wording.

## The Will to Power
Always identify it as a posthumous editorial compilation of notebook entries. The current site may use its numbered entries as a comparative notebook route, but never describe it as a finished Nietzsche book or use it to override published works without argument.

## Tone test
Before publishing, ask:
- Does this sound like a guide to inquiry rather than a verdict?
- Can the reader verify the interpretation from the citation?
- Have we preserved complications?
- Have we named the strongest tension rather than smoothing it away?
- Have we avoided making Nietzsche sound more contemporary, therapeutic, democratic, or systematic than the text warrants?
