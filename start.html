Your first reading: the madman

Allow about 20–30 minutes. You need only one short passage: The Gay Science §125. You can read it online or in your own copy.

Open the reading: §125

Thomas Common’s translation calls the book The Joyful Wisdom. Find section 125, “The Madman.” Return here after reading it once.

Before you read

A man enters a marketplace carrying a lantern in daylight. The people around him already do not believe in God. Read slowly enough to notice who is speaking and how the audience reacts.

The main question: why would an announcement about God unsettle people who already consider themselves unbelievers?

Three things to notice

  1. What does the lantern suggest about looking for something in an apparently well-lit world?
  2. Why does the madman speak of something people have done, rather than simply arguing that a belief is false?
  3. What could it mean for an event to have happened while people have not yet grasped its consequences?

A way to read the passage

The guide reads the death of God as a crisis in the authority of inherited values. Giving up a religious belief may be easier than understanding how deeply a culture’s ideas of truth, duty, and purpose depend on it. On this reading, the audience’s laughter shows a gap between disbelief and understanding.

This is an interpretation to test against the images and dramatic action. The madman is a character in the passage; do not assume every sentence is a straightforward statement in Nietzsche’s own voice.

Check your understanding

Why does the audience matter?

Because its members already disbelieve. Their failure to understand suggests the passage concerns the consequences of a cultural change, rather than merely announcing atheism.

Does the passage prove that nothing has value?

No. It raises the problem of what happens to inherited sources of value. Whether and how new valuations are possible requires further reading.

What should I write in my notes?

In two or three sentences, explain the difference between abandoning a belief and understanding its consequences. Then identify one image in the passage that supports your explanation and one question you still have.
